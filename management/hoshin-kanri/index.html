    Hoshin kanri¶

    Vergil does a large share of its work unattended, and the faster development goes, the more the small instabilities in that work show. The fast, ad hoc engineering work needs a management cadence beside it: a fixed set of reviews at the start and end of each day, and at week, month, quarter and year boundaries, each leaving a written record in the logbook. This page explains the model those reviews follow, so a later reader understands why they are shaped as they are.

    The decision behind it: until a better framework is found, Vergil scales 方針管理 hōshin kanri down to one person working with agents, and adopts its practices in spirit where not to the letter. The skill, its documents and the logbook use the method's Japanese terms freely, in content. Vergil is a personal, single-user stack that takes no outside contributions, so the vocabulary is chosen for its one reader.

    The method¶

    The word. 方針 hōshin is policy or direction; the characters are literally "direction" and "needle", as in a compass needle. 管理 kanri is management. In English it is usually called policy deployment.

    Origin. It grew out of Japan's total quality control movement in the late 1950s and 1960s, building on Deming's plan–do–check–act cycle and Drucker's management by objectives. Bridgestone Tire is usually credited with naming and first using it in 1965 (one history dates the company's idea to systematize it to 1962); Komatsu and Toyota were early adopters. Hewlett-Packard brought it to the US in the 1980s, and Danaher made it central to the Danaher Business System.

    How it works.

    1. A three-to-five-year direction yields a small number of yearly breakthrough objectives: the vital few, deliberately not a long list.
    2. Each level turns the objectives above it into its own targets and the means of reaching them.
    3. キャッチボール catchball: targets and means pass back and forth between levels until both sides agree they are achievable. Direction comes from the top; the means are agreed with the people doing the work.
    4. Two halves: hōshin, which drives change toward the direction, and 日常管理 nichijō kanri (daily management), which keeps normal operation stable and standardised. Hōshin assumes daily management is under control.
    5. A nested review cadence (monthly, quarterly), and a yearly review by top management, 社長診断 shachō shindan (the president's diagnosis). Each review's findings feed the next cycle's plan.

    Terms¶

    Term In Vergil
    方針管理 hōshin kanri The whole system; the skill is /vergil:hoshin.
    日常管理 nichijō kanri The daily review, run on every invocation.
    朝礼 chōrei The daily start review (morning assembly).
    終礼 shūrei The daily end review (closing assembly).
    方針 hōshin The direction document, hoshin.md, holding the vital few.
    反省 hansei Reflection; the end review at the week, month and quarter levels.
    社長診断 shachō shindan The yearly end review; in a one-person company the president is the human.
    キャッチボール catchball Agreeing objectives and means; the agent proposes, the human adjusts, until both accept.
    改善 kaizen Notes, in each entry, on improving the reviews themselves.

    The two halves¶

    • Nichijō kanri (daily) runs on every invocation. start is the 朝礼 chōrei: what ran unattended since the last review, what failed and why, and what needs action today. end is the 終礼 shūrei: what changed today, what is waiting on the human, and a note for tomorrow's chōrei.
    • Hōshin (week, month, quarter, year) runs only at boundaries, or when the human asks to close a period with end <level>. A start review sets direction for the period; an end review is 反省 hansei, reflection on the period just finished. The yearly end is the 社長診断 shachō shindan.

    Every review ends with a kaizen prompt: what should this review do differently?

    The calendar in brief¶

    • One command each morning. /vergil:hoshin start works out for itself which reviews are due, closes whatever period was left open, and runs them. /vergil:hoshin end [<level>] is optional: plain end closes the day, and end week, end month, end quarter or end year closes that period and every smaller one still open beneath it.
    • One time zone. Every date and time is computed in the IANA time zone recorded in the logbook, never from the host clock.
    • Days. A day with a start entry is a day worked; a day with none is a day off, and nothing about it is flagged.
    • Weeks run Monday to Sunday, and every period comes from the week: a week belongs to the month its Monday falls in, and so to that month's quarter and year. The week of Monday 2025-12-29 belongs to December 2025, Q4 2025 and 2025, so January 2026, Q1 2026 and 2026 begin with the week of Monday 2026-01-05.
    • Order. Closing reviews run first, smallest level first, since reflection rolls up. Then the daily chōrei runs, so operations are known to be stable before direction is set, and the boundary start reviews follow from the largest level down, since direction flows top-down.
    • Nothing silently skipped. A period opened and never closed is offered for closing at the next start: run the missed end review late, or record it as not performed. At the week level and above, periods with no entries at all are named as gaps in the next start entry.
    • Windows. Each review covers a window from the end of the last reviewed window, so a skipped day widens the next window rather than losing time.
    • The first run on an empty logbook opens every level, and sets the first vital few by catchball.

    Scaled to one person and agents¶

    In a company, hōshin kanri runs through layers of management, and catchball passes between them. Vergil has one person and the agents working with them, so the method is adopted in spirit where not to the letter:

    • The human is every level of management. The yearly shachō shindan is the human's own review of the year; there is no one above them to report to.
    • Catchball is between the human and the agent. The agent proposes objectives, focus and means from the durable record; the human adjusts until both accept.
    • The agent does the gathering. Each review's data comes from durable state (git and GitHub today), through vrg-* tools where they exist. Every check the skill runs by hand is written as the brief for a tool that will later do it deterministically.
    • The cadence fits the human's working day. start is run on most working days, weekends included, and carries the boundaries; end is the human's choice, because the day ends when it ends.
    • The record is public and light. The logbook is Markdown with a minimal header, published on this site, and nothing a security record keeps private appears in it.
    • Kaizen improves the reviews themselves. Each entry's kaizen notes feed back into the skill, so the first, deliberately thin checklists grow through use.
